Routine upkeep, long-term replacements and overdue assessments require different decisions. Keep their purposes and procedures separate.

## Essentials

- Identify how the association’s reserve accounts were established and the requirements governing them. Labeling money “reserves” does not answer every legal question about its use or funding.
- Connect replacement planning with actual assets, remaining life, expected costs and existing balances. Do not import condominium structural-reserve requirements into a single-family HOA by assumption.
- Use the applicable assessment and collection process. Required notices and legal steps matter; a friendly reminder and a statutory demand are not interchangeable.
- Have qualified counsel handle uncertain lien, foreclosure or disputed-account questions. Track communications and provide accurate account information.

## Key mistake

Using reserve money for ordinary operating bills without checking the rules governing that reserve.

## Board discussion

A board wants to use the pond-repair reserve to pay ordinary mowing bills because several owners are late with assessments.

- What governs use of that reserve?
- What collection steps are appropriate before assuming the cash shortage can be solved by moving funds?

Review the reserve’s legal status and applicable authorization requirements before using it for another purpose. Separately follow the assessment and collection process. Convenience does not determine whether a transfer or collection action is permitted.

## Self-paced work

What determines whether pond reserves can pay mowing bills?

- The reserve’s legal status and applicable authorization requirements
- The convenience of the transfer
- A director calling it temporary

Answer: The reserve’s legal status and applicable authorization requirements

Establish how reserves were created and what governs their use before moving funds.

How should overdue assessments be handled?

- Skip the notices because the HOA needs cash
- Apply condominium procedures automatically
- Follow the applicable assessment and collection process, using counsel where needed

Answer: Follow the applicable assessment and collection process, using counsel where needed

Reserve use and collections are separate decisions. Statutory notices and the legal process cannot be replaced by convenience.

Explain the reserve question to resolve, a separate collection step to verify, and the evidence the board should retain.

## Knowledge check

Are all reserve accounts automatically governed by condominium SIRS rules?

- Yes
- No
- Only in December

Answer: No

Determine the law and reserve status applicable to the HOA.

Can an informal reminder replace every required collection notice?

- Yes
- Only by unanimous agreement
- No

Answer: No

Follow the applicable statutory and governing-document process.
