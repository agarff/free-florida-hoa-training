A consistent process is easier to explain and defend than a series of improvised exceptions. Identify the rule, facts and remedy before moving to fines.

## Essentials

- Confirm that a restriction is enforceable and applies to the facts. Preserve objective evidence and distinguish an observation from an assumption. Consider the effect of amendments and protected rights.
- Separate a courtesy reminder, a board decision and an independent hearing committee’s role. The statutory fining committee has eligibility requirements; the board cannot assume it can fill every role itself.
- Section 720.305 specifies notice, hearing, cure and communication requirements. Read the current procedure before setting a hearing or collecting a fine. Old association policies may need updating.
- Use counsel for disputed interpretation, selective-enforcement questions, litigation and applicable presuit procedures. Do not use education discussion as an unannounced decision on a resident’s case.

## Key mistake

Fining first and checking authority, notice, hearing requirements and consistency afterward.

Use a properly constituted committee and the statutory procedure. Document the violation and the steps taken. The board’s belief that a violation is obvious does not eliminate procedural requirements.

## Self-paced work

### Levying fines: the basic sequence

Under §720.305(2), ordinary limits are $100 per violation and $1,000 total unless governing documents provide otherwise. Give at least 14 days’ written hearing notice; hold the hearing within 90 days before at least three eligible committee members, excluding directors, officers, employees and specified relatives. The committee confirms or rejects the board’s proposed fine by majority. Send findings within seven days. A qualifying cure prevents imposition. An approved uncured fine’s payment date must allow at least 30 days after the findings notice. Check all exceptions and the current statute before acting.

May the directors appoint themselves to approve their own proposed fine?

- No; check the statutory committee eligibility and independent role

Keep the board’s action separate from the statutory fining committee’s role and eligibility rules.
