A useful vendor comparison begins with scope and authority, not just price. Insurance and contract terms should fit the work and the association’s responsibilities.

## Essentials

- Write a comparable scope: location, deliverables, exclusions, timing, cleanup, permits where applicable and who accepts the work. Tree trimming and tree removal are different purchases.
- Section 720.3055 generally requires competitive bids when covered contract payments exceed ten percent of the annual budget including reserves, subject to its exceptions. It does not require accepting the lowest bid.
- Check contract duration, renewal, termination, insurance, licensing where required, indemnity and payment terms. Governing documents may impose additional approval limits.
- Disclose director interests in a vendor and follow the applicable interested-transaction process. Keep the proposals, decision and signed agreement in the records.

## Key mistake

Choosing the cheapest proposal before checking that the vendors are pricing the same work.

## Board discussion

One contractor offers $350 to trim a tree. Another offers $1,000 to remove it. The board describes the cheaper proposal as an equivalent bid.

- What differences in scope should the decision acknowledge?
- What qualifications and insurance would you check?

Decide the intended outcome and compare scope as well as price. Explain whether preservation or removal is appropriate using qualified information where needed. Record the actual selected work and spending authority.

### Trash contract: who must approve?

A board unanimously approves a budgeted three-year trash contract. The HOA’s Articles of Incorporation require prior homeowner approval for covered contracts longer than one year. The president plans to sign this covered contract without that approval.

What must happen before signing, and what record would show the homeowners properly approved the contract?

Hold the signature until homeowners approve, unless an applicable exception permits signing without prior approval. Verify the applicable meeting and notice procedures, quorum and voting requirements. Keep the approval record, including the minutes and vote result. Budget funding, a unanimous board vote and signing authority do not replace required member approval. The one-year threshold comes from this HOA’s Articles, not a statewide requirement for every multi-year contract. If already signed, ask association counsel to assess authority and advise on corrective action. Do not assume the contract is automatically invalid or that a later vote automatically cures the problem.

## Self-paced work

What should the tree-work decision compare first?

- Only the two prices
- The intended outcome and the trimming/removal scopes
- Which contractor advertised first

Answer: The intended outcome and the trimming/removal scopes

Trimming and removal produce different outcomes. Define the authorized work before comparing price.

What should happen before the selected work begins?

- Record authority, scope and the relevant contract/insurance checks
- Pay without a written scope
- Treat the informal quote as unlimited authority

Answer: Record authority, scope and the relevant contract/insurance checks

Check who can authorize and sign, the actual work, qualifications and applicable insurance, then keep the decision record.

What should the president do about the trash contract if no exception applies?

- Sign because the board voted unanimously
- Wait for homeowner approval through the required process
- Sign now and rely on a later vote

Answer: Wait for homeowner approval through the required process

Verify the applicable meeting, quorum and voting requirements, then keep the approval record before signing.

Explain the intended tree-work outcome and one contract or insurance check. For the trash contract, describe how you would verify homeowner approval and what record you would keep.

## Knowledge check

Must the board always accept the lowest bid?

- Yes
- No
- Only if it is half the price

Answer: No

The statute does not require the lowest bid; scope and suitability matter.

What belongs in a useful comparison?

- Price only
- Scope, qualifications, terms, authority and price
- The vendor’s advertising slogan

Answer: Scope, qualifications, terms, authority and price

Compare the work actually being purchased.

The Articles require homeowner approval for this trash contract. No exception applies. When may the president sign?

- After the unanimous board vote
- After homeowners approve through the required process
- As soon as funding is budgeted

Answer: After homeowners approve through the required process

Budgeting, board approval and signing authority cannot substitute for the required homeowner vote.
