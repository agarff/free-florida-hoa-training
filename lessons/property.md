Maintenance planning begins with knowing who owns, operates and must maintain each asset. A nearby pond or landscape strip is not automatically the HOA’s responsibility.

## Essentials

- Create an asset-and-responsibility map using deeds, easements, permits, contracts and master-association documents. Separate owner, neighborhood HOA, master association and government obligations.
- Inspect drainage structures, trees, irrigation, entry lighting and other relevant assets through qualified people. Keep maintenance and incident records; do not alter permitted stormwater systems casually.
- Build a storm plan covering contacts, contractor availability, records backup, insurance notice and immediate hazards. Emergency authority under §720.316 has conditions and is not a substitute for ordinary procedures at all times. Keep an insurance calendar. Review D&O as well as property, general liability and fidelity/crime coverage with qualified advisers. Section 720.3033(5) separately requires insurance or a fidelity bond for persons controlling funds, subject to its annual member-waiver procedure; this is not the same as D&O.
- For wildlife concerns, distinguish state species-specific feeding restrictions from the association’s own proposed or adopted rules. Use current FWC guidance and qualified assistance.

## Key mistake

Waiting for a storm or loss before deciding who maintains the asset and who handles the response.

## Board discussion

Water is backing up near a pond before a storm. A resident asks the board to lower the pond by cutting a new drainage channel.

- Who is the permitted operator and responsible maintenance party?
- What makes an improvised modification risky?

Identify the responsible operator and use qualified emergency and permitting guidance. Do not assume that ownership nearby gives authority to modify a permitted drainage system. Document the report, response and escalation.

## Self-paced work

What must be established before modifying pond drainage?

- Whether most residents prefer a lower level
- Whether a director lives nearby
- Who operates the permitted system and what authority/conditions govern it

Answer: Who operates the permitted system and what authority/conditions govern it

Responsibility and permitting come from the applicable records, not proximity or a neighborhood preference.

How should the board respond to an immediate drainage concern?

- Cut a new channel immediately without review
- Contact the responsible operator and qualified emergency/permitting help
- Assume emergency powers remove all limits

Answer: Contact the responsible operator and qualified emergency/permitting help

Use qualified guidance and the conditions on emergency authority. Record the report, escalation and response.

Name the records you would locate, the responsible contact to identify, and how you would document the response.

## Knowledge check

How should maintenance responsibility be established?

- By proximity alone
- By the applicable documents, permits and records
- By a neighborhood poll

Answer: By the applicable documents, permits and records

Use the actual allocation of responsibility.

Are emergency powers unlimited year-round?

- Yes
- No
- Only for landscaping

Answer: No

Emergency authority has statutory conditions and limits.
