Insurance supports a careful board; it does not replace sound decisions. Learn which policy may respond, what to ask the broker, and why renewal and claim-reporting details matter.

## Essentials

- Distinguish the policies. D&O addresses covered governance allegations. General liability addresses covered injury or property damage; property insurance protects scheduled association assets; fidelity/crime addresses covered theft or dishonesty. These policies do not automatically substitute for one another.
- Read the actual D&O coverage. Check insured people and entities, defense costs inside or outside the limit, deductibles, exclusions, prior-acts dates, nonmonetary claims and relevant architectural, discrimination or contract allegations. The policy and endorsements control coverage.
- Keep legal duties separate. Section 720.3033(5) requires insurance or a fidelity bond for people controlling funds, subject to its annual member-waiver procedure. This is not a general D&O mandate. Check additional duties in your documents; volunteer status and indemnification do not guarantee insurance coverage.
- Control renewals, reporting and broker authority. Calendar renewal and claim-reporting contacts. Claims-made policies can have strict notice conditions; report a demand promptly under the policy. Read a broker-of-record letter before signing: it changes carrier representation, not necessarily coverage or price.

## Key mistake

Assuming a volunteer role, a generic liability policy or a cheaper premium guarantees protection for the board.

## Board discussion

The HOA receives a lawyer’s letter challenging a fence decision one week before insurance renewal. A new broker promises a lower price and asks for a broker-of-record letter. Nobody has compared the D&O terms or reporting deadlines.

- What should the board report now, and which policy details must it check?
- What authority and service terms should it understand before changing brokers?

Follow the current policy’s notice instructions promptly and retain evidence of reporting. Ask the broker to compare insured parties, limits, defense costs, exclusions, prior-acts and reporting terms. Review broker authority before signing. Keep the decision and policy documents; do not assume a cheaper quote or a volunteer role removes exposure.

## Self-paced work

What is the immediate response to the lawyer’s letter?

- Follow the current policy’s reporting instructions and preserve the letter
- Wait for a lawsuit
- Assume the renewal policy will cover it

Answer: Follow the current policy’s reporting instructions and preserve the letter

Claims-made reporting conditions can be strict. Report promptly under the policy and seek coverage guidance.

What should the broker comparison include?

- Premium alone
- A promise that volunteers cannot be liable
- Insured parties, defense costs, limits, exclusions and reporting terms

Answer: Insured parties, defense costs, limits, exclusions and reporting terms

Policy terms and endorsements determine the coverage. Also understand a broker-of-record letter before signing it.

Identify a reporting action, two coverage details to compare, and who would follow up with the broker.

## Knowledge check

Does D&O insurance automatically replace fidelity/crime coverage?

- Yes
- No
- Only for a small HOA

Answer: No

The coverages address different risks. Check §720.3033(5), your documents and the actual policy terms.

A demand arrives just before renewal. What is the best first step?

- Wait until a lawsuit is filed
- Report it promptly as the current policy requires and preserve the record
- Assume the next policy will cover it

Answer: Report it promptly as the current policy requires and preserve the record

A claims-made policy can impose strict reporting conditions. The broker and insurer should evaluate the actual notice and coverage terms.
